# Photography — what Waseem has, and what it still needs

Every image on this site is Waseem Jewellers' own. Nothing has been generated,
retouched into something it is not, or borrowed. Where a photograph is below the
standard the rest of the site sets, it has been **framed smaller and more
carefully** rather than enlarged to fill a slab — but framing cannot add
resolution, remove a mirror reflection, or turn a catalogue cut-out into a
portrait. This is the honest record of where new photography would change the
result most.

Ranked by how much a new shoot would improve the page.

## 1 — Needed before launch

### Royal Wedding · Polki Raani Haar (`p01`) and Rukh-e-Jana · Pleated Gold Collar (`p02`)

**The gap: there is no jewellery photography for these two pieces at all.**
All three gallery frames on each page are campaign portraits, and the largest
source is 1080 px. On a product page the model's face carries the frame and the
piece is small within it — the opposite of what a product page is for.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sources | `p01-hero` 1080×1080, `p01-second` 960×960, `p01-third` 1080×1080 (all campaign)<br>`p02-hero` 1080×1080, `p02-second` 960×960, `p02-third` 1080×1080 (all campaign) |
| Done here | The opening frame stays tall; the two answering frames are now square and crop closer to the neckline, so the jewellery leads. Neither is ever shown full-bleed. |
| Needed | A macro of the choker and of the raani haar's drop, and a still-life of the set laid out. 2400 px or larger. The same for the pleated collar and its kundan leaf closure. |

## 2 — Below flagship standard, usable meanwhile

### Lavender Halo Ring (`p09`) — the piece the review singled out

The three frames are studio cut-outs on white with a mirrored reflection beneath.
The retouching is heavy: the stone's facets are over-sharpened, the prongs carry
a halo, and the colour differs between frames (lavender in the three-quarter
view, distinctly pink in the front view). At the old full-column size these
faults were the first thing the eye met.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sources | `p09-hero` 2000×2000, `p09-second` 2000×2000, `p09-macro` 1000×1000 — all packshot |
| Done here | Mounted on a pearl plate with real margin: the ring renders at **473 px** across a 739×591 plate instead of filling 739×900. The processing is far less obtrusive at that size, and the piece reads as an object rather than a cut-out. |
| Needed | Re-shoot on a warm ground with a single soft key and no mirror; keep one macro at 2400 px. Consistent white balance across frames. |

### Emerald Tassel Earrings (`p08`)

Same studio-cut-out convention with a mirrored reflection. Better than `p09` —
the emeralds and the tassel read well — but it is catalogue photography.

| | |
|---|---|
| Sources | `p08-hero` 2000×2000, `p08-macro` 1000×1200 — both packshot |
| Done here | Mounted on the pearl plate at 473 px; the tassel has room to hang. |
| Needed | One worn frame, or a still-life on cloth, so the drop's movement is visible. |

## Already at standard

**Naqsh-e-Gul Pearl Blossom Choker** (`p03`, 2880 + two macros at 2000 and 1530)
and **Sapphire Pendant Suite** (`p07`, 2880 + macros at 1760 and 1440) need
nothing. They are the benchmark the rest should be shot against.

## Also absent

No archival photography from Waseem exists in any form. The heritage chapter
is built from the showroom facade, a gold vitrine, a kundan macro and campaign
stills. A 1952-era photograph, a portrait of Chaudhry Muhammad Afzal, or an early
workshop image would carry that chapter far better than anything framing can do.

## The rule this site follows

- A photograph is framed by what it is: a campaign portrait opens tall and its
  answers sit square; a macro takes the band its subject asks for; a studio
  cut-out is mounted on a plate with margin.
- No image is enlarged past what its negative carries.
- No specification is shown that Waseem has not verified for that piece.
